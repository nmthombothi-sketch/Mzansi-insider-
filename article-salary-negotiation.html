How to Negotiate Your Salary in South Africa: Scripts, Tactics and Red Lines

Most South Africans leave 15–30% of potential earnings on the table at the offer stage. Here is a step-by-step framework with exact scripts for every scenario — new job, promotion, and counter-offer.

By Thabo Mokoena, Career & Finance Analyst | Updated: September 7, 2026

Never Name the First Number

When asked your salary expectations early in the process, deflect gracefully to market research: "Based on my research for this role and my specific experience, I'm looking in the range of R[X] to R[Y]." Anchor high, but ensure the number is defensible with data.

Know Your Market Rate

Never negotiate based on hope. Use the Mzansi Insider salary benchmarks, PayScale, and local industry reports (like OfferZen's annual tech report). Walk into the room with a number backed by hard data.

Salary Negotiation Checklist at a Glance

Phase Key Action
Pre-Interview Research market rates and determine your absolute minimum (walk-away number).
The Offer Express gratitude, ask for 24 hours to review, and never accept on the spot.
The Counter Use a collaborative script, anchor 10–15% above your target, and highlight specific value.
The Close Get the final agreed-upon number and benefits in writing before resigning from your current role.
"The candidate who says 'I'd like to discuss the package' almost always ends up better paid than the one who says 'that sounds fine, thank you.'"

The New-Offer Script

When countering a new job offer, keep it collaborative, not confrontational: "Thank you, I'm incredibly excited about the role and the team. The offer is a little below what I was expecting given my proven experience with [specific skill/achievement]. Is there flexibility to move the base salary to R[X]?" Then, stop talking. Let the silence do the heavy lifting.

Negotiating a Raise

Build your case weeks before the conversation. Document your wins, tie them directly to revenue generation or cost savings, and book a dedicated meeting rather than ambushing your manager. Frame the request as: "I'd like to discuss aligning my compensation with the expanded scope and measurable value I'm delivering."

Red Lines

  • ❌ Never bluff a competing offer you don't actually have. Reputation damage is permanent.
  • ❌ Never make it personal (e.g., "I need more money because my rent went up"). Keep it strictly about market value and business impact.
  • ❌ Never accept on the spot if you feel pressured. "Can I review this and come back to you tomorrow morning?" is a universally respected and professional response.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I negotiate my salary in South Africa?
Research market rates using local salary guides, never name the first number, anchor high but defensibly, and use a collaborative script like 'Is there flexibility to move the base to R[X] given my experience?'
What is a good salary increase to ask for?
For a new job, aim for a 10–20% increase from your previous salary, or align with the market rate for the role. For internal promotions or annual reviews, 5–15% is standard, depending on performance and inflation.
Should I accept the first salary offer?
Rarely. Most employers build a 10–15% negotiation buffer into their initial offers. Always ask for 24 hours to review the package and counter professionally.
How do I negotiate a salary increase in my current job?
Document your wins, tie them directly to revenue generation or cost savings, book a dedicated meeting, and frame the request as aligning your package with the value you are delivering.

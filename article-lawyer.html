How to Become a Lawyer in South Africa: Attorney vs Advocate

The legal profession splits into two distinct paths — attorney and advocate — each with very different training routes, lifestyles, and earning potential. Here's how to qualify for both.

By Thabo Mokoena, Career & Finance Analyst | Updated: September 7, 2026

Start with the LLB

The non-negotiable foundation for any legal career in South Africa is a four-year LLB (Bachelor of Laws) degree. Alternatively, some students complete a BA or BCom in Law followed by a two-year LLB. This is the common starting point for both future attorneys and advocates.

Legal Career Path at a Glance

Career Stage Duration Estimated Earnings
LLB Student 4 years N/A (Often covered by bursaries/NSFAS)
Candidate Attorney / Pupil 1–2 years R12,000 – R25,000 / month
Admitted Attorney / Junior Advocate Ongoing R350,000 – R800,000+ / year
Senior Partner / Senior Counsel (SC) 10+ years post-admission R1.5 million – R5 million+ / year

Attorney or Advocate?

Attorneys deal directly with clients, manage legal matters, and complete a two-year articles (training contract) at a law firm. Advocates are specialist courtroom litigators who complete a year of pupillage at the Bar. They are briefed by attorneys and operate as referral counsel. The two paths carry very different risks, rewards, and daily lifestyles.

Admission to the Roll

Both routes require passing the rigorous Legal Practice Council (LPC) board exams and being formally admitted by the High Court. Only after this formal admission can you legally practise as an independent legal professional.

"Big Law partners and senior advocates are among the best-paid professionals in the country — but the early years, especially an advocate's pupillage, can pay very little."

What Lawyers Earn

Legal pay is highly polarised. While candidate attorneys earn a modest stipend, those who make it to partnership in large commercial firms or achieve Senior Counsel (SC) status command top-tier salaries. See our lawyer salary guide and our becoming an attorney guide for detailed breakdowns.

Get It Funded

Law degrees are expensive, but funding is available. The Department of Justice Law Bursary fully funds LLB students in exchange for a mandatory work-back commitment in the state justice system. Major commercial law firms also offer lucrative bursaries.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does it take to become a lawyer in South Africa?
An LLB takes four years, followed by two years of articles (for attorneys) or a year of pupillage (for advocates), plus passing the board exams and being admitted by the High Court.
What's the difference between an attorney and an advocate?
Attorneys deal directly with clients and complete articles; advocates are courtroom specialists who complete pupillage at the Bar and are briefed by attorneys.
How much do lawyers earn in South Africa?
Pay is highly polarised — candidate attorneys earn a modest stipend, while Big Law partners and senior advocates are among the best-paid professionals. See our lawyer salary guide.
Can you get a bursary to study law?
Yes. The Department of Justice Law Bursary funds LLB students in exchange for a work-back commitment in the justice system.

About This Learnership

The Welding and Metal Fabrication Learnership, facilitated through the merSETA, is a highly practical 18-month programme designed to train the next generation of skilled artisans. It covers essential metal fabrication techniques, welding safety protocols, and blueprint reading, leading directly to an NQF Level 3 qualification.

Unlike many white-collar roles, skilled trades like welding and boilermaking offer strong, durable earnings that cannot be easily outsourced or automated. This learnership provides a direct, debt-free pathway into the construction, mining, and manufacturing sectors, culminating in the opportunity to write the national artisan trade test.

Eligibility Requirements

  • Citizenship: Must be a South African citizen with a valid ID document.
  • Education: Grade 10, 11, or 12 certificate. While Mathematics and Physical Science are a strong advantage, many programmes accept candidates without them if they demonstrate strong practical aptitude.
  • Age: Typically targeted at unemployed youth between the ages of 18 and 35.
  • Physical Fitness: Must be physically capable of performing hands-on workshop and site work, which may involve lifting, standing for long periods, and working in varying conditions.
  • Attributes: A genuine interest in engineering and fabrication, coupled with a strict adherence to health and safety protocols.

Required Documents Checklist

Before applying, ensure you have clear, certified copies (not older than 3 months) of the following documents ready:

  • ✅ Certified copy of your South African ID document
  • ✅ Certified copy of your highest educational qualification (Grade 10, 11, or 12 certificate/statement of results)
  • ✅ Proof of residence (e.g., utility bill or sworn affidavit)
  • ✅ An updated, professional CV highlighting any manual, technical, or workshop-related experience
  • ✅ A brief motivational letter explaining your interest in welding, metal fabrication, and the artisan trade

Stipend & What You Get

This learnership is designed to support you financially while you master a highly specialized, hands-on trade. Over the 18-month period, you will receive:

  • Monthly Stipend: Approximately R5,000 per month to assist with transport, tools, and living expenses during the training period.
  • Formal Qualification: An accredited merSETA NQF Level 3 qualification in Welding and Metal Fabrication, recognized nationwide.
  • Practical Experience: Hands-on workshop and site experience, learning to read technical drawings, operate welding equipment (MIG, TIG, ARC), and perform quality checks.
  • Trade Test Pathway: The logbook hours and theoretical knowledge gained serve as the mandatory foundation for writing the national Artisan Trade Test to become a qualified Boilermaker or Welder.

Expert Tips for a Winning Application

Standing out in the welding and fabrication learnership pool requires demonstrating reliability, physical readiness, and a genuine interest in the trade:

  • Highlight Practical Experience: Mention any informal experience with manual labor, metalwork, automotive repair, or technical school projects.
  • Emphasize Safety: Workshops and construction sites have zero tolerance for safety violations. Mention your understanding of the importance of PPE and following procedures.
  • Show Attention to Detail: Welding requires precision. Provide examples of tasks you've completed that required high accuracy and careful measurement.
  • Proofread Everything: Ensure your CV and motivational letter are neat, professional, and completely free of errors, demonstrating your professionalism.

Career Opportunities After Completion

Completing this learnership and passing your trade test opens the door to highly respected and well-compensated career paths in the industrial sector:

  • Welder or Metal Fabricator: Working in manufacturing plants, automotive shops, or construction sites, building and repairing metal structures.
  • Boilermaker: With additional experience and passing the trade test, specializing in the construction and maintenance of boilers, pressure vessels, and heavy industrial equipment.
  • Workshop or Production Technician: Overseeing quality control, reading complex blueprints, and managing fabrication workflows.
  • Site Artisan in Mining or Construction: Traveling to remote sites to perform critical maintenance and fabrication work, often commanding premium site allowances.
  • Self-Employed Contractor: Starting your own mobile welding or custom metal fabrication business once you have gained sufficient experience and industry contacts.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does the welding learnership pay? +
It pays a monthly stipend of around R5,000 over an 18-month period, plus a merSETA NQF Level 3 qualification and a pathway toward an artisan trade test.
Can you earn well as a welder without a degree? +
Yes. Skilled trades like welding and boilermaking can pay strong, durable wages without a university degree, especially after passing a national trade test.
Do you need matric for a welding learnership? +
Many welding learnerships accept Grade 10 to 12. While Mathematics and Physical Science are a strong advantage, they are not always strictly essential for entry-level fabrication roles.
Where do you find welding learnerships? +
They are advertised by companies funded by the merSETA, on corporate careers pages, and via official SETA portals throughout the year.

About This Learnership

The Basic Traffic Officer Learnership is a rigorous 18-month government-funded programme run by provincial departments of transport and metropolitan municipalities. It trains unemployed youth to become qualified traffic officers, combining intensive classroom theory, physical training, and practical road-traffic law enforcement experience.

Upon successful completion, learners are absorbed as permanent traffic officers in the public sector, gaining access to stable government employment, medical aid, pension benefits, and a clear career progression path within South Africa's law-enforcement services.

Eligibility Requirements

  • Citizenship: Must be a South African citizen with a valid ID document.
  • Education: A certified Grade 12 (Matric) certificate.
  • Driver's Licence: A valid driver's licence (Code B / Code 8 or higher) is strictly mandatory.
  • Age: Typically targeted at unemployed youth between the ages of 18 and 35.
  • Physical Fitness: Must pass a strict physical fitness test and occupational medical examination.
  • Criminal Record: Must have a completely clear criminal record (no convictions, including traffic-related offences).
  • Discipline: Willingness to undergo rigorous physical, disciplinary, and paramilitary-style training.

Required Documents Checklist

Before applying, ensure you have clear, certified copies (not older than 3 months) of the following documents ready:

  • ✅ Certified copy of your South African ID document
  • ✅ Certified copy of your Grade 12 / Matric certificate
  • ✅ Certified copy of your valid driver's licence (Code B or higher)
  • ✅ Proof of residence (e.g., utility bill or sworn affidavit)
  • ✅ An updated, professional CV
  • ✅ Completed Z83 form (for government posts) — available from any government department
  • ✅ A brief motivational letter explaining your interest in law enforcement and public service

Stipend & What You Get

This learnership is designed to support you financially while you undergo intensive training to become a qualified law-enforcement officer. Over the 18-month period, you will receive:

  • Monthly Stipend: Approximately R5,500 per month to assist with transport and living expenses during the training period.
  • Formal Qualification: An accredited NQF Level 4 qualification in Traffic Officer / Road Traffic Law Enforcement, recognized nationwide.
  • Intensive Training: Classroom-based learning in traffic law, combined with physical fitness training, defensive driving, and practical road-patrol experience.
  • Career Pathway: Direct absorption into permanent public-sector employment as a qualified traffic officer, with access to government medical aid, pension, and housing benefits.

Application Process

Step by step
1
Find the Opening: Monitor provincial government websites, municipal career pages, and the national DPSA vacancy portal for traffic officer learnership adverts.
2
Complete the Z83: Fill out the official Z83 application form for government posts accurately and completely.
3
Submit Documents: Submit your Z83, certified ID, Matric certificate, driver's licence, CV, and motivational letter to the specified address or email.
4
Assessments: Shortlisted candidates will undergo physical fitness tests, medical examinations, and strict background/integrity checks.
5
Interview & Onboarding: Successful candidates attend a panel interview and, if selected, sign a learnership agreement to begin the 18-month training programme.

Expert Tips for a Winning Application

Standing out in the traffic officer learnership pool requires demonstrating discipline, integrity, and a genuine commitment to public service:

  • Start Physical Training Early: The physical fitness test is demanding. Begin running, push-ups, and sit-ups well before your application to ensure you pass the assessment.
  • Ensure a Clean Record: Any criminal conviction, even minor traffic offences, will disqualify you. Resolve any outstanding fines or legal issues before applying.
  • Highlight Discipline: Mention any experience in structured environments such as military service, sports teams, martial arts, or volunteer work that demonstrates discipline and teamwork.
  • Complete the Z83 Perfectly: Government applications are rejected for incomplete forms. Ensure every section of the Z83 is filled out accurately and signed.
  • Show Community Commitment: Express a genuine desire to serve your community and improve road safety in your motivational letter.

Career Opportunities After Completion

Completing this learnership opens the door to a stable and respected career in public-sector law enforcement:

  • Qualified Traffic Officer: Enforcing traffic laws, conducting roadblocks, and issuing fines on provincial or national roads.
  • Provincial or Metro Traffic Officer: Working for a specific province (e.g., Gauteng, Western Cape) or metropolitan municipality (e.g., City of Johannesburg, City of Cape Town).
  • Road Safety Officer: Focusing on public education, accident prevention, and road safety campaigns.
  • Law-Enforcement Support Roles: Transitioning into specialized units such as K9, forensic collision investigation, or VIP protection with further training.
  • Progression Within Services: Advancing to senior ranks such as Chief Traffic Officer, with corresponding salary increases and leadership responsibilities.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does the traffic officer learnership pay? +
It pays a monthly stipend of around R5,500 over the 18-month programme, plus you earn a nationally recognised traffic-officer qualification.
Do you need a driver's licence for the traffic officer learnership? +
Yes. A valid driver's licence (typically Code B / Code 8 or higher) is mandatory, along with physical fitness and a clear criminal record.
Who can apply for the traffic officer learnership? +
South African citizens with a Grade 12 / Matric, a valid driver's licence, who are physically fit, have a clear criminal record, and are aged roughly 18–35.
When does the traffic officer learnership close? +
Intakes vary by province and municipality, often closing mid-year (around April). Always confirm the exact date on the relevant provincial or municipal government site.
Do I get a permanent job after the learnership? +
Yes. Successful completion of the learnership typically leads to permanent absorption as a qualified traffic officer in the public sector, with full government benefits including medical aid and pension.

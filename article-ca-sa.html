How to Become a CA(SA): The Complete Timeline, Cost and Return on Investment

South Africa's most prestigious accounting qualification takes 6–8 years from matric to completion. Is the investment in time and fees worth it? We model the numbers from article clerk to CFO.

By Thabo Mokoena, Career & Finance Analyst | Updated: September 7, 2026

The Path in Brief

Becoming a CA(SA) means completing an accredited BCom Accounting degree (3 years), a postgraduate CTA or Honours (1–2 years), and then a three-year SAICA training contract (articles) at an accredited firm, all while passing the rigorous Initial Test of Competence (ITC) and Assessment of Professional Competence (APC) board exams.

CA(SA) Timeline & ROI at a Glance

Stage Duration Estimated Earnings / Cost
BCom Accounting + CTA 4–5 years Cost: R350k – R550k (often covered by bursaries)
Articles (Training Contract) 3 years Salary: R18,000 – R28,000 / month
Newly Qualified CA(SA) Year 1 post-articles Salary: R600,000 – R800,000 / year
CFO / Senior Finance Leader 10+ years post-articles Salary: R1.75 million – R4 million+ / year

Step 1: Accredited Undergraduate Degree

Study a SAICA-accredited BCom Accounting at universities such as UCT, Stellenbosch, Wits, UP, or UKZN. Aim to pass every subject on the first attempt — failing a core module can add a full year to your timeline and delay your entry into the workforce.

Step 2: CTA / Honours

The Certificate in the Theory of Accounting (CTA) is widely considered the toughest academic year of the journey, with notoriously high failure rates. Many students repeat it. Budget your time and finances accordingly, or secure a bursary that covers postgraduate study.

Step 3: Articles and Board Exams

This is a mandatory three-year training contract at an audit or commerce firm. You earn a salary during this period, which significantly offsets the earlier costs of your degree. You must pass the ITC after completing your CTA, and the APC near the end of your articles, to qualify.

"The CA(SA) is expensive and brutal, but it remains the single most reliable ticket to the executive suite in South African business."

The Return on Investment

For those who complete the grueling process, the return on investment is exceptionally strong. The qualification opens doors to finance leadership, investment banking, and the boardroom faster than almost any other undergraduate route in the country.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does it take to become a CA(SA)?
It typically takes 6 to 8 years from matric to completion: a 3-year accredited BCom, 1 year of CTA/Honours, and a 3-year SAICA training contract (articles), alongside passing the ITC and APC board exams.
How much does it cost to become a CA(SA)?
Total tuition for a BCom and CTA ranges from R350,000 to R550,000. However, this is often offset by bursaries (like Thuthuka) and the salary earned during your 3-year articles.
What are the ITC and APC exams?
The ITC (Initial Test of Competence) is written after completing your CTA, and the APC (Assessment of Professional Competence) is written near the end of your articles. Both are rigorous professional exams set by SAICA.
Is the CA(SA) qualification worth it?
Financially, yes. Newly qualified CA(SA)s typically earn R600,000–R800,000 per year, with CFO and senior finance roles reaching R1.75m–R4m+ annually, offering a strong return on investment.

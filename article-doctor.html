How to Become a Doctor in South Africa: MBChB, Specialisation, and Public vs Private Practice

From matric requirements to community service, registrar posts, and the financial reality of a South African medical career — everything prospective doctors need to know.

By Thabo Mokoena, Career & Finance Analyst | Updated: September 7, 2026

Matric Requirements

You'll need strong results in Mathematics, Physical Sciences, and Life Sciences. Most medical schools expect a minimum of 70% across these key subjects, but intense competition pushes the real cut-off much higher. Additionally, a strong score on the National Benchmark Test (NBT) is universally required.

Medical Career Path at a Glance

The MBChB Degree

This is a rigorous six-year Bachelor of Medicine and Surgery degree offered at one of South Africa's accredited medical schools (UCT, Wits, Stellenbosch, UP, UKZN, UFS, Sefako Makgatho, or Walter Sisulu). Places are strictly limited and heavily oversubscribed every year.

Internship and Community Service

After graduating, you must complete two years of supervised internship, followed by one year of compulsory community service ("comm serve") in the public health system. This is often spent in rural or under-resourced areas. Only after completing these three years can you register with the Health Professions Council of South Africa (HPCSA) as an independent practitioner.

"The training is long and the public-sector hours are punishing, but few careers offer the same combination of job security, profound meaning, and long-term earning potential."

Specialisation

To specialise, you must compete for a highly sought-after registrar post and complete four to six further years of rigorous training, culminating in college fellowship exams. Specialties like surgery, cardiology, and radiology are the most competitive to enter — and consequently, the most lucrative.

The Financial Reality

Private practice pays substantially more than the public sector, but it carries significant business risk, administrative burdens, and overheads. As a result, many doctors choose to blend public-sector job security with private sessional work to maximize their income and flexibility.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does it take to become a doctor in South Africa?
It takes a minimum of 9 years: 6 years for the MBChB degree, 2 years of internship, and 1 year of compulsory community service before full HPCSA registration.
What matric subjects do you need for medical school?
You need strong results in Mathematics, Physical Sciences, and Life Sciences. Most medical schools expect a minimum of 70% across these key subjects, plus a strong National Benchmark Test (NBT) score.
What is community service for doctors in South Africa?
After internship, doctors must complete one year of compulsory community service, typically in a public hospital or clinic, often in rural or under-resourced areas, to qualify for independent practice.
Do doctors earn more in private practice?
Yes. While public sector salaries are stable, private practice offers substantially higher earning potential, though it comes with business risks and overheads.

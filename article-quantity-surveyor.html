How to Become a Quantity Surveyor in South Africa

Quantity surveyors are the financial managers of construction. Here's how to qualify, register as a PrQS, and build a well-paid built-environment career.

By Thabo Mokoena, Career & Finance Analyst | Updated: September 7, 2026

What a QS Does

Quantity surveyors manage the cost and commercial side of construction projects — estimating, budgeting, and controlling costs from tender to completion. It's a highly respected profession that pairs technical construction knowledge with sharp commercial and financial acumen.

Quantity Surveyor Career Path & Pay Scale at a Glance

Career Stage Duration / Requirement Estimated Earnings
BSc (Hons) QS Student 4 years N/A (Often covered by bursaries)
Candidate Quantity Surveyor 2–3 years post-grad R250,000 – R400,000 / year
Professional QS (PrQS) Registered with SACQSP R450,000 – R750,000+ / year
Senior QS / Commercial Manager 5–10+ years experience R800,000 – R1.5 million+ / year

The Qualification

Study a BSc (Hons) in Quantity Surveying or Construction Economics at an accredited South African university (such as UCT, Wits, UP, or UJ). Strong Mathematics skills are highly beneficial, as the day-to-day work is heavily numbers-driven and requires precise attention to detail.

Professional Registration

After completing your degree and a candidate phase gaining practical experience, you register as a Professional Quantity Surveyor (PrQS) with the South African Council for the Quantity Surveying Profession (SACQSP). This registration is the key earnings driver and unlocks senior commercial and consulting roles.

"Registration as a PrQS is the milestone that separates a graduate salary from a senior commercial one — it's a credential worth pursuing early in your career."

Who Employs QSs

Quantity surveyors are in high demand across the built environment. Major construction contractors like WBHO and Murray & Roberts, specialist cost-consultancies, and large property developers are the primary employers. See our QS salary guide for detailed earning ranges.

Get Started

Apply for a BSc QS programme at an accredited university, and consider securing a construction learnership to gain valuable early site exposure and industry connections.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do you become a quantity surveyor in South Africa?
Study a BSc (Hons) in Quantity Surveying or Construction Economics, complete a candidate phase gaining experience, then register as a Professional Quantity Surveyor (PrQS) with the SACQSP.
Does PrQS registration increase pay?
Yes. Registration as a Professional Quantity Surveyor with the SACQSP is a major pay driver and unlocks senior commercial and consulting roles.
Who employs quantity surveyors?
Major construction contractors such as WBHO and Murray & Roberts, along with cost-consultancies and property developers.
Is quantity surveying a good career?
Yes. It offers stable, well-paid work tied to construction and property, with earnings rising meaningfully after professional registration.

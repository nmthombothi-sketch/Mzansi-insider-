Delivery & E-hailing in South Africa

One of the lowest-barrier side hustles in South Africa — if you have a bicycle, scooter, or car and a smartphone, you can start earning within days.

Overview

Delivery platforms like Bolt Food, Uber Eats, and Mr D actively recruit delivery partners across most major cities and many towns in South Africa. E-hailing driving with Uber or Bolt pays more but requires a roadworthy car, a valid PrDP (Professional Driving Permit), and the relevant operating licenses.

The appeal is flexibility and a fast start: you choose your own hours, and the onboarding process is significantly quicker compared to traditional employment or other business ventures.

How to Start

Step by step
1
Choose your platform: Bolt Food, Uber Eats, or Mr D for delivery; Uber or Bolt for e-hailing driving.
2
Gather your documents: SA ID, valid driver’s licence (and PrDP for driving), proof of residence, and banking details.
3
Register on the platform's app or website and complete the onboarding and vehicle roadworthiness checks.
4
Start during peak busy periods (evenings, weekends, and rainy days) to maximise your earnings and tips.
5
Track your fuel, data, and maintenance costs meticulously so you know your real profit, not just your gross turnover.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much can you earn delivering in South Africa?
Part-time food delivery realistically brings R3 000–R8 000 a month, while full e-hailing driving with a car earns roughly R5 000–R15 000 a month after costs.
What do you need to start delivery work?
A bicycle, scooter, or car, a smartphone, and the platform's required documents. E-hailing driving also needs a valid PrDP licence and a roadworthy, registered vehicle.
Do delivery costs eat into earnings?
Yes. Fuel, data, and vehicle wear reduce your gross, so it's important to track real profit rather than just turnover.
Can you do delivery without a car?
Yes. Many platforms allow bicycle or scooter delivery, and you can also lease your vehicle to a vetted driver for more passive income.

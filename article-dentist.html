How to Become a Dentist in South Africa: Degree, Registration and Pay

Dentistry combines healthcare with the earning power of running a practice. Here's the definitive path from school to private practice or specialisation.

By Thabo Mokoena, Career & Finance Analyst | Updated: September 7, 2026

The Degree

Becoming a dentist starts with a five-year Bachelor of Dental Surgery (BChD) or equivalent at an accredited university with a dental school (such as UCT, Wits, UP, Sefako Makgatho, or UWC). Entry is highly competitive and demands exceptional matric results, particularly in Mathematics and Physical Science.

Dentist Career Path at a Glance

Career Stage Duration Estimated Earnings
BChD Student 5 years N/A (Often covered by bursaries/NSFAS)
Community Service Dentist 1 year R35,000 – R45,000 / month
Registered Dentist (Private Practice) Ongoing R60,000 – R120,000+ / month
Specialist (e.g., Orthodontist) 3–4 years additional study R120,000 – R250,000+ / month

Community Service and Registration

After graduating, you must complete a year of compulsory community service, typically at a public hospital or clinic. Following this, you register with the Health Professions Council of South Africa (HPCSA). This registration is mandatory and must be maintained annually to practise legally.

Where the Money Is

Private practice dominates dentist income in South Africa. The highest earners own their own practices or specialise in high-demand fields like orthodontics, oral surgery, or maxillofacial surgery. See our dentist salary guide for detailed earning potential.

"Owning a practice is what turns a good dental salary into a great one — clinical skill combined with business acumen is the ultimate winning combination."

Specialising

Specialisation requires a BChD degree, usually two years of general practice experience, and then 3–4 years of full-time university study. It's a long and rigorous road, but it offers a significantly higher earning ceiling and the ability to handle complex cases.

Get Started

Apply to an accredited dental school, secure funding early through NSFAS or a dedicated healthcare bursary, and focus intensely on the Mathematics and Science marks that gate entry.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does it take to become a dentist?
Six years in total: a five-year BChD degree plus one year of compulsory community service before full HPCSA registration.
Do dentists need to register with the HPCSA?
Yes. Dentists must register with the Health Professions Council of South Africa (HPCSA) to practise, after completing their degree and community service.
Do dentists earn more in private practice?
Yes. Private practice dominates dentist income, and owning a practice or specialising in fields like orthodontics pushes earnings to the top.
What subjects do you need to study dentistry?
Excellent matric results in Mathematics and Physical Science are essential, as entry to dental school is highly competitive.

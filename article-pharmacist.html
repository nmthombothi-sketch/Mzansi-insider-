How to Become a Pharmacist in South Africa

Pharmacy is a scarce skill with steady demand across retail, hospital, and industry. Here's the definitive path from BPharm to registered pharmacist.

By Thabo Mokoena, Career & Finance Analyst | Updated: September 7, 2026

The BPharm Degree

Becoming a pharmacist starts with a four-year Bachelor of Pharmacy (BPharm) degree at an accredited South African university. Strong marks in Mathematics and Physical Science are typically required for entry into these highly competitive programmes.

Pharmacist Career Path & Pay Scale at a Glance

Career Stage Duration Estimated Earnings
BPharm Student 4 years N/A (Often covered by NSFAS/bursaries)
Intern & Community Service 2 years total R250,000 – R350,000 / year
Registered Pharmacist Ongoing R350,000 – R600,000+ / year
Pharmacy Owner / Industry Specialist 5+ years post-registration R600,000 – R1.2 million+ / year

Internship and Community Service

After completing your degree, you must complete a one-year internship followed by a one-year community service placement. This structured, supervised experience is mandatory before you can register and practise independently.

Register with the SAPC

Registration with the South African Pharmacy Council (SAPC) is mandatory to practise. The SAPC is the regulatory body that licenses pharmacists, sets professional standards, and ensures patient safety across the country.

"Pharmacy ownership and the pharmaceutical industry — not just retail and hospital roles — are where the highest pharmacist earnings sit."

Where Pharmacists Work

Pharmacists are employed across retail chains (like Clicks and Dis-Chem), public and private hospitals, and the broader pharmaceutical industry. Ownership and industry roles (such as medical affairs or regulatory affairs) typically offer the highest earning potential. See our pharmacist salary guide for detailed breakdowns.

Get Started

Apply for a BPharm programme at an accredited university and secure funding early through NSFAS or a dedicated healthcare bursary to avoid student debt.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does it take to become a pharmacist?
Six years in total: a four-year BPharm degree, a one-year internship, and a one-year community service placement before full registration.
Do pharmacists register with the SAPC?
Yes. Registration with the South African Pharmacy Council (SAPC) is mandatory to practise as a pharmacist in South Africa.
Where do pharmacists earn the most?
The pharmaceutical industry and pharmacy ownership offer the highest earnings, followed by hospital and retail management roles.
Is pharmacy a scarce skill?
Yes. Pharmacy is recognised as a scarce-skill profession with steady demand across retail, hospital, and industry settings.

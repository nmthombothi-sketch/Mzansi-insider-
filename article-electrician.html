How to Become an Electrician in South Africa: Trade Test and Beyond

Electrical work is a strong-earning trade you can enter without a university degree — and specialists can out-earn many graduates. Here's the practical path.

By Thabo Mokoena, Career & Finance Analyst | Updated: September 7, 2026

Why the Trades Make Sense

Not everyone needs a degree to earn well. Electrical work is in steady, high demand thanks to ongoing infrastructure, construction, and renewable energy projects. Qualified specialists can earn very comfortably. It's hands-on, practical, and far cheaper to train for than a traditional university route, often with zero student debt.

Electrician Career Path at a Glance

Career Stage Duration / Requirement Estimated Earnings
Apprentice / Learner 3–4 years (TVET + on-the-job) R5,000 – R12,000 / month
Trade-Tested Electrician Passed RED SEAL trade test R15,000 – R25,000 / month
Master Electrician / Wireman Wireman's Licence + experience R30,000 – R50,000+ / month
Specialist / Business Owner Industrial, solar, or own contracting firm R50,000 – R100,000+ / month

The Apprenticeship Route

The most common path is an apprenticeship: working under a qualified, registered electrician while learning the trade, often combined with theoretical blocks at a TVET college. This earns you the logged hours and practical knowledge you'll need to attempt the trade test.

The Trade Test

Becoming a qualified electrician means passing a formal trade test (often referred to as achieving your "Red Seal"), which certifies your competence. This is the key milestone that separates apprentices from qualified, well-paid tradespeople.

"A trade-tested electrician with a wireman's licence and a specialisation can earn more than many degree-holding professionals — without the student debt."

The Wireman's Licence

To certify electrical installations as compliant with South African standards (SANS 10142-1), you'll want a wireman's licence. This raises your earning power significantly, as it legally allows you to sign off on Certificate of Compliance (CoC) documents that uncertified electricians cannot.

Specialise to Earn More

The highest earners specialise in high-demand niches like industrial automation, high-voltage systems, or solar and renewable energy installations. Mining and heavy industrial sites also pay a substantial premium for skilled electrical artisans. See our electrician salary guide for detailed earning ranges.

Get Started

Look for an apprenticeship with a registered employer or enrol in an electrical engineering programme at a local TVET college. A construction or technical learnership can also be a highly useful entry point to get your foot in the door.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do you become a qualified electrician in South Africa?
Through an apprenticeship with a registered employer or a TVET college pathway, followed by a trade test that certifies your competence as a qualified electrician.
Can you earn good money as an electrician without a degree?
Yes. Trade-tested electricians who specialise (industrial, high-voltage, solar) or hold a wireman's licence can earn R40,000 to R60,000 or more per month at senior level.
What is a wireman's licence?
A wireman's licence allows you to certify electrical installations as compliant. It raises your earning power because you can sign off work that uncertified electricians cannot.
How long does it take to become an electrician?
It varies, but several years of apprenticeship and on-the-job experience are typical before you're ready to pass the trade test and qualify.

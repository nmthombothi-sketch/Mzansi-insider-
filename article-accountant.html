How to Become an Accountant in South Africa: CA(SA) and Beyond

Accounting is one of the most reliable routes to strong, stable earnings — and the CA(SA) is its gold standard. Here are all the routes in.

By Thabo Mokoena, Career & Finance Analyst | Updated: September 7, 2026

More Than One Designation

"Accountant" covers several professional routes. The most prestigious is the CA(SA) via SAICA, but there are also routes through SAIPA (professional accountant), CIMA (management accounting), and ACCA. Each suits different career goals, lifestyles, and timelines.

Accounting Routes at a Glance

Designation Governing Body Best For
CA(SA) SAICA Audit, executive leadership, and top-tier finance roles
Professional Accountant (SA) SAIPA SME accounting, tax, and general practice
CIMA / ACCA CIMA / ACCA Management accounting and global corporate finance

The CA(SA) Route

This means completing a SAICA-accredited BCom Accounting degree, followed by a postgraduate qualification (CTA or Honours). After that, you must complete a three-year training contract (articles), usually at an audit firm, and pass the rigorous professional exams: the Initial Test of Competence (ITC) and the Assessment of Professional Competence (APC). It's demanding, but it opens the most doors in the South African corporate landscape.

Articles and Earning Potential

Your training contract is where theory meets practice. Pay is modest during articles (typically R15,000–R25,000 per month), but it rises sharply once you qualify. Newly qualified CA(SA)s can expect R45,000–R65,000+ per month, with senior roles scaling much higher. See our accountant salary guide and our full CA(SA) guide for detailed breakdowns.

"The CA(SA) is one of the most versatile qualifications in the country — it opens doors well beyond accounting, into finance leadership and the C-suite."

Get It Funded

Studying doesn't have to mean crippling debt. The SAICA Thuthuka Bursary fully funds qualifying African and Coloured students toward the CA(SA) designation, providing comprehensive tuition, accommodation, and personal academic support.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do you become a CA(SA)?
A SAICA-accredited BCom Accounting degree, then a postgraduate CTA/Honours, a three-year training contract (articles), and the professional exams (ITC and APC) set by SAICA.
Are there other accounting designations?
Yes. Besides the CA(SA) via SAICA, there are routes through SAIPA (professional accountant), CIMA (management accounting), and ACCA, each suiting different goals.
How much do accountants earn in South Africa?
Pay rises sharply after qualifying. Article clerks typically earn R15k–R25k/month, while newly qualified CA(SA)s can expect R45k–R65k+ per month, with financial managers earning even more.
Can you get a bursary to study accounting?
Yes. The SAICA Thuthuka Bursary fully funds qualifying students toward the CA(SA), with academic and personal support.

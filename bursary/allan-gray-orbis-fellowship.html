About This Bursary

The Allan Gray Orbis Foundation Fellowship is widely regarded as one of the most prestigious and comprehensive student development programmes in Southern Africa. Unlike traditional bursaries that simply pay tuition fees, this Fellowship is a long-term investment in high-potential young South Africans who demonstrate exceptional entrepreneurial "spark."

The Foundation's mission is to cultivate responsible, high-impact entrepreneurs who will eventually create jobs, drive innovation, and contribute positively to the African economy. Fellows don't just earn a degree; they undergo a rigorous, multi-year development journey encompassing mentorship, leadership training, and character development.

Eligibility Requirements

  • Citizenship: Must be a South African citizen (Fellowships are also available for Namibian, Botswanan, and Eswatini citizens via the EBN Fellowship).
  • Age Limit: You must be under the age of 21 in the year of application (typically Grade 12 learners).
  • Academic Excellence: A proven track record of academic excellence, typically requiring an A or B aggregate with strong performance in Mathematics and English.
  • Entrepreneurial Spirit: You must demonstrate genuine entrepreneurial potential, initiative, and a desire to create value in your community.
  • Field of Study: Must intend to study at a recognized South African university in an approved faculty (Commerce, Science, Engineering, Law, Humanities, Arts, or Health Sciences).
  • Exclusions: The Foundation strictly excludes Medicine, Dentistry, Veterinary Science, and certain specialized academic tracks.
  • Commitment: Willingness to actively participate in the Foundation's intensive development programme throughout your degree.

Comprehensive Benefits (What It Covers)

The Fellowship is a "full-cost" plus "high-value development" package, easily valued at well over R100,000 per annum.

  • Financial Coverage: 100% of university tuition fees, residence/accommodation fees, meals, prescribed books, and a laptop allowance.
  • Living Stipend: A monthly living allowance to cover personal expenses and transport.
  • Development Programme: Access to exclusive entrepreneurship bootcamps, leadership workshops, and life-coaching sessions.
  • Mentorship: Pairing with established business leaders and Allan Gray Orbis alumni.
  • Post-Grad Support: Access to seed funding opportunities, incubation hubs, and the powerful Orbis global network after graduation.

The Multi-Stage Selection Process

Because this Fellowship is incredibly competitive, the selection process is rigorous and spans several months.

1
Online Application (Closes 30 April 2026): Submit your academic results, ID, and motivational essays via the official Allan Gray Orbis portal.
2
Assessments (May - July): Shortlisted candidates undergo online psychometric profiling and cognitive assessments.
3
Panel Interviews (August - September): Candidates who pass the assessments are invited to rigorous panel interviews with Foundation staff and alumni.
4
National Selection Camp (September - October): Finalists attend a multi-day, high-pressure "Selection Camp" involving group tasks, simulations, and final evaluations to determine the final cohort.
5
Final Offer: Successful candidates accept the Fellowship offer and join the development programme before starting university.

Expert Tips for a Winning Application

Standing out in the Allan Gray Fellowship pool requires more than just good grades. Here is how to elevate your application:

  • Show, Don't Just Tell: Don't just say you are an entrepreneur. Provide concrete examples of initiatives you've started, problems you've solved in your community, or small businesses you've run.
  • Understand the "Orbis" Philosophy: Read up on the Foundation's core values. They look for humility, integrity, and a desire to serve the greater good, not just a desire to get rich.
  • Prepare for the Psychometrics: The Foundation uses specific cognitive and behavioral assessments. Practice logical reasoning and self-reflection beforehand.
  • Secure Strong References: Choose referees (teachers, community leaders) who can speak specifically to your character, resilience, and leadership, not just your grades.

Life After the Fellowship: The Orbis Network

Graduating as an Allan Gray Fellow is just the beginning. Alumni join the Orbis community, a powerful network of founders and leaders across the continent. Many Fellows go on to launch successful startups, secure top-tier graduate roles in corporate strategy, or establish non-profit organizations. The Foundation continues to support its alumni through its "Chairs in Entrepreneurship" at major universities and various enterprise development initiatives.

Frequently Asked Questions

What makes the Allan Gray Fellowship different from a normal bursary?
Beyond full financial coverage, it is an intensive, multi-year entrepreneurship and leadership development programme. It provides mentorship, character development, and lifelong access to the powerful Orbis alumni network, aiming to create job-creators rather than just job-seekers.
Can I study Medicine or Dentistry with this Fellowship?
No. While the Fellowship covers Commerce, Science, Engineering, Law, Arts, and Health Sciences, it strictly excludes Medicine, Dentistry, and Veterinary Science. These fields do not align with the Foundation's specific entrepreneurship mandate.
Do I need to own a registered business to apply?
No, a registered company is not required. The Foundation looks for 'entrepreneurial spark' and potential. School initiatives, community projects, informal trading, or solving local problems demonstrate the initiative they seek.
How competitive is the selection process?
Extremely competitive. It involves a multi-stage assessment including psychometric testing, panel interviews, and a rigorous multi-day National Selection Camp. Only a small cohort of top-tier students is selected annually.
What happens if my marks drop during my degree?
Fellows must maintain a minimum academic average (typically 65% or higher) and actively participate in the Foundation's development programme to retain their funding and status year-on-year.
When does the application close for the 2026 intake?
The official closing date for the University Fellowship (Grade 12 applicants) is 30 April 2026. High School Scholarship applications (Grades 6-11) typically close later in the year, around August/September.

NSFAS — National Student Financial Aid Scheme 2026

South Africa's largest government student funding scheme, covering the full cost of study at public universities and TVET colleges for students from low-income households — no repayment while you meet the conditions.

About This Bursary

The National Student Financial Aid Scheme (NSFAS) funds qualifying South African students at all 26 public universities and TVET colleges. It is means-tested: funding is aimed at students from households earning below the annual threshold. Unlike a traditional loan, NSFAS funding does not have to be repaid as long as you remain academically eligible and meet the scheme's conditions, making it the backbone of access to higher education in South Africa.

Requirements

  • Citizenship: Must be a South African citizen.
  • Income Threshold: Combined household income must be below the NSFAS threshold (typically R350,000 per year; higher for students with recognized disabilities).
  • Institution: Must be accepted or applying to a public university or TVET college.
  • Academic Progression: Must meet the academic progression requirements (passing a minimum number of modules) to retain funding in subsequent years.

What It Covers

  • Full Tuition Fees: 100% coverage of registered course fees.
  • Accommodation/Transport: Full accommodation allowance (if living in accredited residence) or a transport allowance for day students.
  • Learning Materials: An annual allowance for books, laptops, and other study essentials.
  • Living Allowance: A monthly stipend paid directly to the student for personal upkeep.

How to Apply

Step by step
1
Create a myNSFAS account on the official portal (my.nsfas.org.za) using your valid SA ID number.
2
Complete the online application form with accurate personal, contact, and household details.
3
Upload required supporting documents: your ID, parents'/guardians' IDs, and proof of household income (e.g., payslips, SARS IT34, or sworn affidavits for unemployed guardians).
4
Provide explicit consent for third-party verification of your household income by SARS and other government databases.
5
Submit your application before the closing date and regularly track your status on the myNSFAS dashboard.

Frequently Asked Questions

Who qualifies for NSFAS in 2026?
South African citizens accepted at a public university or TVET college whose combined household income is below the NSFAS threshold (typically around R350 000 per year, and higher for students with disabilities).
Does NSFAS have to be repaid?
NSFAS funding does not have to be repaid as long as you remain academically eligible and meet the scheme's conditions. It functions as a bursary rather than a loan for qualifying students.
What does NSFAS cover?
NSFAS covers full tuition, accommodation or a transport allowance, a learning materials allowance, and a monthly living allowance.
When do NSFAS applications close?
Applications for the 2026 academic year typically close towards the end of the preceding year — confirm the exact date on the official myNSFAS portal, as it changes annually.

About This Bursary

The Department of Justice and Constitutional Development (DoJ&CD) Law Bursary is a prestigious government initiative designed to transform the legal landscape of South Africa. It funds high-potential students studying towards a Bachelor of Laws (LLB) at accredited South African universities.

In return for comprehensive financial support, recipients commit to a work-back agreement within the justice system. This helps build crucial capacity in courts, the National Prosecuting Authority (NPA), the State Attorney's office, and other vital legal services, offering graduates a direct and stable career path in public service.

Eligibility Requirements

  • Citizenship: Must be a South African citizen with a valid ID document.
  • Academic Excellence: Strong Matric results (particularly in English and critical subjects) or an excellent academic record if already studying towards an LLB.
  • Institutional Acceptance: Must be formally accepted or currently registered for an LLB degree at a recognized South African university.
  • Financial Need: Demonstrated financial need is a primary consideration, as the bursary aims to support disadvantaged but talented students.
  • Work-Back Commitment: Willingness to sign a contract committing to work for the Department of Justice or related state entities for a period equal to the duration of the bursary support.

Required Documents Checklist

Before starting your application, ensure you have the following certified documents ready to upload or submit:

  • ✅ Certified copy of your South African ID document
  • ✅ Certified copy of your latest academic results (Matric certificate or official university transcript)
  • ✅ Proof of university acceptance or current registration for the LLB program
  • ✅ Proof of household income (e.g., parents' payslips, SASSA grant proof, or a sworn affidavit if unemployed)
  • ✅ A detailed motivational letter explaining your passion for law and public service
  • ✅ A comprehensive CV highlighting academic achievements, leadership roles, and community involvement

What It Covers

The DoJ Law Bursary is a comprehensive package designed to remove financial barriers so you can focus entirely on the rigorous demands of law school:

  • Financial Coverage: 100% of university tuition and registration fees.
  • Living Expenses: University residence accommodation or approved private accommodation, plus meals.
  • Study Materials: Allowance for prescribed law textbooks, study guides, and a laptop.
  • Monthly Stipend: A living allowance to cover personal expenses and transport.
  • Career Pathway: Practical exposure (e.g., vacation work) and a direct path to permanent employment in the public legal sector upon successful qualification.

Expert Tips for a Winning Application

Standing out in the government bursary pool requires careful preparation and a clear demonstration of your potential:

  • Highlight Public Service: The DoJ values candidates committed to societal transformation. Mention any volunteer work, community leadership, or advocacy experience.
  • Understand the Work-Back: Show that you have researched and genuinely understand the work-back obligation. This demonstrates maturity and long-term commitment.
  • Certify Everything: Ensure all copies of your ID, results, and other documents are certified by a Commissioner of Oaths within the last 3 months.
  • Write a Compelling Motivational Letter: Don't use a generic template. Explain specifically why you want to study law and why you want to serve in the public justice system.
  • Keep Copies: Always keep a complete, stamped copy of your submitted application for your own records.

Life After the Bursary: Career Opportunities

Qualifying as a DoJ-funded lawyer opens the door to a stable, impactful, and rewarding career in the public sector:

  • Guaranteed Employment: Fulfill your work-back obligation with a guaranteed post as a State Attorney, State Prosecutor, or Legal Advisor in a government department.
  • Practical Experience: Gain invaluable, high-volume litigation and advisory experience early in your career, accelerating your professional development.
  • Further Specialization: The Department often supports further academic or professional progression, such as funding your articles of clerkship or specialized legal training.
  • Job Security & Benefits: Enjoy the stability of public-sector employment, complete with government medical aid, pension contributions, and structured salary progression.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does the Department of Justice law bursary cover? +
It typically covers full tuition, registration, accommodation, prescribed books, and a monthly living allowance for LLB students, plus practical exposure within the justice system.
Is there a work-back obligation? +
Yes. In return for funding, recipients commit to serving in the justice system (e.g., as a state attorney or legal advisor) for a set period, usually equal to the duration of the bursary support, after qualifying.
Who can apply for the law bursary? +
South African citizens who are registered or accepted for an LLB degree at an accredited South African university, with strong academic results and demonstrated financial need.
When does the Department of Justice law bursary close? +
Applications usually close in the second half of the year (around August to October). Always confirm the exact date on the official Department of Justice and Constitutional Development website.
Can I apply if I am already in my second or third year of LLB? +
Yes, current LLB students can often apply, provided they are in good academic standing and meet the financial need criteria. Check the specific year-of-study limits on the application form.

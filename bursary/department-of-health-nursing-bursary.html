About This Bursary

The Department of Health (DoH) Nursing Bursary is a critical government initiative designed to address South Africa's nursing shortage by funding students at accredited nursing education institutions. It covers the cost of study plus a monthly stipend, and in return, recipients commit to a work-back agreement, serving in public health facilities for a set period after qualifying.

This bursary is an excellent opportunity for students passionate about healthcare who want a guaranteed career path in the public sector, along with financial security during their studies.

Eligibility Requirements

  • Citizenship: Must be a South African citizen with a valid ID document.
  • Academic Excellence: Strong Matric results, particularly in Life Sciences, Mathematics/Mathematical Literacy, and English.
  • Institutional Acceptance: Must be accepted or applying to an accredited nursing education institution (e.g., public nursing colleges or universities).
  • Medical Fitness: Must be medically fit to practice as a nurse, as required by the South African Nursing Council (SANC).
  • Work-Back Commitment: Willingness to sign a contract committing to work in the public health sector for a period equal to the duration of the bursary support.
  • Financial Need: While merit is important, demonstrated financial need is often a favorable factor in the selection process.

Required Documents Checklist

Before starting your application, ensure you have the following certified documents ready:

  • ✅ Certified copy of your South African ID document
  • ✅ Certified copy of your latest academic results (Matric certificate or university transcript)
  • ✅ Proof of acceptance or application to an accredited nursing institution
  • ✅ Proof of household income (e.g., parents' payslips or an affidavit if unemployed)
  • ✅ A motivational letter explaining your passion for nursing and public service
  • ✅ SANC registration or proof of application (if applicable)

What It Covers

The DoH Nursing Bursary is designed to remove financial barriers so you can focus entirely on your demanding studies:

  • Financial Coverage: 100% of tuition and registration fees at the accredited institution.
  • Study Materials: Allowance for prescribed textbooks, nursing equipment, and uniforms.
  • Monthly Stipend: A living allowance to cover personal expenses, transport, and accommodation (if not provided by the college).
  • Clinical Placement: Guaranteed practical training placements in public hospitals and clinics.
  • Career Pathway: A direct path to permanent public-sector nursing employment upon successful qualification.

Expert Tips for a Winning Application

Standing out in the government bursary pool requires careful preparation:

  • Apply in Your Home Province: Provincial Departments of Health prioritize candidates who are residents of that province and intend to work in local facilities.
  • Highlight Community Service: Nursing is a service-oriented profession. Mention any volunteer work, caregiving experience, or community involvement in your motivational letter.
  • Understand the Work-Back: Show that you have researched and genuinely understand the work-back obligation. This demonstrates maturity and commitment.
  • Certify Everything: Ensure all copies of your ID, results, and other documents are certified by a Commissioner of Oaths within the last 3–6 months.
  • Keep Copies: Always keep a complete, stamped copy of your submitted application for your own records.

Life After the Bursary: Career Opportunities

Qualifying as a DoH-funded nurse opens the door to a stable and rewarding career in the public health sector:

  • Guaranteed Employment: Fulfill your work-back obligation with a guaranteed post in a public hospital or clinic, complete with government benefits.
  • Specialization: After gaining experience, you can pursue postgraduate diplomas in specialized fields like ICU Nursing, Theatre Nursing, Midwifery, or Psychiatric Nursing.
  • Further Studies: The DoH often supports further academic progression, such as bridging from a Diploma to a Bachelor of Nursing (BCur) degree.
  • Job Security: Nursing remains one of the most in-demand and secure professions in South Africa, with opportunities for overtime and shift allowances.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does the nursing bursary cover? +
It typically covers tuition, registration, uniforms, and a monthly stipend, along with clinical placement during your training.
Is there a work-back obligation? +
Yes. In return for funding, recipients commit to working in the public health system for a set period (usually equal to the duration of the bursary) after qualifying, helping address the national nursing shortage.
What subjects do I need for a nursing bursary? +
Strong results in Life Sciences and related subjects are important, along with meeting the specific admission requirements of the accredited nursing education institution.
Where do I apply for the Department of Health nursing bursary? +
Applications are usually handled through provincial Departments of Health and accredited nursing colleges. Always check your specific provincial health department website for current details and deadlines.
Can I apply if I am already studying nursing? +
Yes, current nursing students can often apply, provided they are in good academic standing and meet the provincial criteria. Check with your specific provincial DoH for year-of-study limits.

How to Become an Actuary in South Africa: Exams, Fellowship and Pay

Actuarial science is among the highest-paid professions in the country — but the road to qualifying is long and exam-heavy. Here's exactly what it takes.

By Thabo Mokoena, Career & Finance Analyst | Updated: September 7, 2026

What Actuaries Do

Actuaries use advanced mathematics, statistics, and financial theory to measure and manage risk — primarily in life insurance, general insurance, pensions, banking, and investments. It's a profession built on extreme rigour, and one of the few where the qualification itself dramatically and predictably lifts your earning power.

The Actuary Career Path at a Glance

Stage Duration Estimated Monthly Earnings
University (BSc Actuarial Science) 3–4 years N/A (Bursary stipends available)
Student Actuary (Working + Exams) 3–6 years R25,000 – R45,000+
Newly Qualified Fellow (FASSA) Ongoing R80,000 – R120,000+

Start with the Right Degree

The standard route is a BSc in Actuarial Science (or a heavy maths/statistics degree) at a recognised university like UCT, Wits, Stellenbosch, or UP. Exceptional matric results — typically 80%+ in pure Mathematics — are essential, as the degree and the professional exams that follow are mathematically intense.

The ASSA Exams

Qualifying means passing a rigorous series of professional exams set by the Actuarial Society of South Africa (ASSA). Students typically write these while working full-time. The exams are structured into foundational, core, and specialist modules. Becoming a Fellow of the Actuarial Society of South Africa (FASSA) usually takes six to ten years in total from the start of university.

"Every set of actuarial exams you pass moves your salary up significantly — but qualifying as a Fellow is a marathon that demands years of disciplined study alongside full-time work."

Get It Funded

Actuarial bursaries are among the most generous in South Africa because the skills are so scarce. The Discovery Actuarial Bursary, along with offerings from major banks and insurers, fully funds top students and provides structured support through the exam process.

Is It Worth It?

Financially, absolutely. Actuaries are consistently among the highest earners in South Africa, with relentless demand from insurers, pension funds, and investment banks. The trade-off is the long, difficult examination process. See our actuary salary guide for detailed earning potential.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does it take to become an actuary in South Africa?
Usually six to ten years: a three-year BSc in actuarial science or maths, followed by a demanding series of professional exams through the Actuarial Society of South Africa, completed while working.
What exams do actuaries write?
Actuaries write a long series of professional exams set by the Actuarial Society of South Africa (ASSA), progressing through foundational, core, and specialist modules toward Fellowship (FASSA).
Is actuarial science worth it?
Financially, yes. Student actuaries often earn R25k–R45k/month, while newly qualified Fellows (FASSA) can command R80k–R120k+ per month, making it one of the highest-paid professions in the country.
What do you need to study actuarial science?
Exceptional matric results, especially in Mathematics (typically 80%+), and acceptance into a BSc Actuarial Science (or maths/statistics) programme at a recognised university.
